# Huddle Design Guidance

## Design Context

### Users

Huddle is designed primarily for small collaborative teams—especially product, design, engineering, agency, and hackathon teams—who need to turn unstructured meeting notes into a clear plan quickly. They are often working under time pressure, switching between conversation and execution, and need immediate confidence about what was captured, where work belongs, and what changed.

The core job is not merely tracking issues. It is transforming a messy team conversation into an organized, editable workflow and keeping that workflow synchronized for everyone.

### Brand Personality

Huddle should feel:

- **Aesthetic:** visually considered, balanced, and quietly distinctive
- **Tasteful:** restrained, intentional, and free of gimmicks
- **Calm:** easy to scan, low-friction, and reassuring during busy work

The interface should evoke clarity and confidence. AI assistance should feel useful and composed rather than magical, noisy, or dominant.

### Aesthetic Direction

- Use Notion as the primary reference for restraint, readable typography, generous whitespace, quiet surfaces, and content-first hierarchy.
- Use a light theme for the initial product and demo.
- Favor warm neutral backgrounds, crisp white work surfaces, ink-like text, subtle borders, and restrained violet/indigo accents.
- Keep density efficient enough for a working Kanban board while preserving breathing room around navigation, headings, and dialogs.
- Use Hugeicons Free in the Stroke Rounded style consistently.
- Use motion sparingly for continuity and feedback, especially when imported cards appear one by one.
- Avoid the visual language of a generic issue tracker: dense tables, heavy administration chrome, excessive badges, rigid enterprise panels, loud status colors, and configuration-first screens.
- Do not imitate Notion literally. Huddle should have its own recognizable real-time board and meeting-import experience.

### Design Principles

1. **Turn conversation into clarity.** The meeting-notes import is the signature experience; make the transformation understandable, visible, and easy to review.
2. **Keep work visually primary.** Navigation and controls should recede until needed so boards, columns, and cards hold attention.
3. **Earn distinctiveness through restraint.** Use excellent typography, spacing, composition, and micro-interactions instead of decorative excess.
4. **Make collaboration feel dependable.** Real-time changes, connection state, permissions, and errors should be clear without becoming distracting.
5. **Support flexible workflows gracefully.** Custom boards and columns should feel natural and approachable, not like administering a complex issue-tracking system.
